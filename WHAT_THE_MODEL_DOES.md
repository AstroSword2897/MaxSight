# What the Model Does (Simple Terms)

**MaxSight** is an AI that helps people with vision (or hearing) difficulties understand their surroundings—like having a calm, clear guide that describes the world in a way that works for you.

---

## In One Sentence

**The model looks at what the camera sees (and optionally hears), figures out what’s there and how important or risky it is, and then describes it in speech, text, or other cues so you can move and make decisions safely.**

---

## What It Actually Does, Step by Step

1. **Looks at the scene**  
   It takes a picture (and optionally sound) from the camera/mic.

2. **Finds and names things**  
   It detects objects (doors, stairs, people, cars, furniture, etc.) and says *what* they are and *where* they are (left, right, center, far/near).

3. **Adds “how important” and “how far”**  
   It labels how urgent something is (e.g. “danger” vs “caution” vs “safe”) and whether it’s near, medium, or far away.

4. **Remembers the environment**  
   Over time it builds a short-term “map” of the space (e.g. “door is usually on the left”) so it can give consistent, useful descriptions.

5. **Adapts to your condition**  
   It can be tuned for different vision conditions (e.g. glaucoma, cataracts, low central vision, color blindness) so the way it emphasizes or describes things fits you better.

6. **Talks to you in more than one way**  
   It can give you:
   - **Speech** (e.g. “Stairs ahead, about 3 steps”)
   - **Text** (e.g. reading signs via OCR)
   - **Visual cues** (e.g. highlights or contrast on a screen)
   - **Haptics** (e.g. vibration patterns for alerts)

7. **Supports practice and routine**  
   It can support simple “skill building” (e.g. contrast or focus tasks) and adapt to how you usually move and what you need day to day.

---

## Simple Analogy

Think of it like a **friendly, knowledgeable companion** who:
- Looks around for you and tells you what’s there  
- Points out hazards and distances  
- Remembers the layout of places you often are  
- Speaks (and can use text or vibration) in a way that’s clear for *you*  
- Adjusts how they describe things based on your vision (or hearing) needs  

The **model** is the “brain” that does the seeing and deciding; the **app** is what turns that into speech, text, and alerts.

---

## What It Does *Not* Do

- It does **not** drive a car or control a vehicle.
- It does **not** replace a doctor or give medical advice.
- It does **not** make decisions for you—it **informs** you so you can decide.
